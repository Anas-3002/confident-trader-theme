METHODOLOGY

Treat the evaluation as a rules-based game

An evaluation is a defined objective under defined constraints. That is a much easier problem than "make money", and it rewards a completely different approach: control the variance, bank progress early, and never let a single session threaten the account.

The three rules that end most challenges

  • Trailing drawdown. A limit that follows your equity high means unrealised profit becomes protected capital. Building a buffer early permanently changes your risk budget.
  • Daily loss limit. Exceeding it is usually terminal. A hard, broker-side limit removed from the decision loop is the only version that survives a bad morning.
  • Consistency requirements. Firms that cap the share of profit from any single day are explicitly telling you that they want a repeatable process, not a lottery ticket.

On vouchers and pass rates

Any figure describing how many candidates pass an evaluation should be treated with scepticism unless it is published by the firm with a stated methodology, and even then it describes a population rather than your specific outcome. The desk does not publish a pass-rate number, and it does not promise funding.
